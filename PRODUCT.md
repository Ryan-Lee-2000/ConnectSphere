# ConnectSphere Product Foundation

## Register

product

## Users

ConnectSphere serves six roles participating in the planning and delivery of client events: Event Organisers, Event Operations Managers, Event Coordinators, Venue Staff, Technical Support Staff, and Attendees. Internal users coordinate time-sensitive work across organisations, venues, equipment, approvals, and event status. Attendees interact with a narrower public-facing registration experience.

Users need to understand their current role, responsibilities, permitted actions, and the state of an event without deciphering the underlying system. The interface should support focused work during an ordinary office day, including periods of deadline pressure, without feeling stressful or bureaucratic.

## Product Purpose

ConnectSphere provides one dependable place to request, plan, approve, resource, and attend events. It should make ownership, status, dependencies, and the next appropriate action clear while protecting each client organisation's information and respecting role-based responsibilities.

Success means a user can enter the product, identify what needs attention, and complete the appropriate task with minimal uncertainty. The system should reduce coordination overhead while remaining trustworthy enough for access control, approvals, bookings, and event changes.

## Brand Personality

**Composed, precise, collaborative.**

ConnectSphere communicates with quiet confidence. Its language is direct and considerate, its workflows are orderly, and its visual character supports cooperation without becoming playful or promotional. Internal surfaces should feel operational and trustworthy; attendee-facing surfaces may be warmer while remaining part of the same product family.

Reference qualities include Stripe Dashboard's trustworthy hierarchy, Skedda's scheduling and approval clarity, Linear's workflow speed and status legibility, and Cvent's breadth across the event lifecycle.

## Anti-references

- Do not use a colourful festival or ticket-sales aesthetic for internal operations.
- Do not imitate Jira's configuration overload, visual clutter, or deeply nested settings.
- Do not inherit the dense legacy-enterprise character associated with large event-management suites.
- Do not use generic purple SaaS gradients, glassmorphism, neon decoration, confetti, or stock event photography as product identity.
- Do not make the entire application resemble a dark developer tool or depend on keyboard-first interaction.
- Do not add decorative dashboards, cards, charts, or motion that do not help a user complete a task.

## Design Principles

1. **Make responsibility unmistakable.** Show the active role, event owner, current state, and available action wherever they affect a decision.
2. **Design for the next operational decision.** Give the most relevant task or status priority over decorative summaries and secondary metadata.
3. **Keep complexity legible.** Use familiar navigation, forms, tables, and status patterns so sophisticated workflows remain understandable.
4. **Earn trust through restraint.** Use precise language, predictable behaviour, and purposeful visual emphasis. Security and permission boundaries must never be ambiguous.
5. **Warm the attendee edge, not the operational core.** Attendee experiences may feel more welcoming, but they should retain the same clarity and accessibility as internal tools.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 Level AA is the design and implementation baseline. Components must support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic structure, programmatic labels, sufficient text and non-text contrast, and errors or statuses that do not rely on colour alone. Motion must be functional, brief, and compatible with reduced-motion preferences. Responsive layouts must preserve task order and meaning rather than merely shrink desktop screens.

This baseline guides implementation from the start; a formal conformance claim requires testing and an audit of the completed product.
